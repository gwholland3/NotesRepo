## Color Coding
Wikipedia: [link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_color_code#Color_band_system)

Consumer resistors have four color bands on them, three on the left and one on the right. From right to left, they are referred to as A, B, C, and D:
- A: 1st band
- B: 2nd band
- C: Multiplier
- D: Tolerance

The 1st band designates the first significant figure of the resistor's resistance. The 2nd band designates the second significant figure.
The 3rd band indicates how many trailing zeroes there are (the power of 10 multiplier) in the resistance, and the tolerance gives you a bound on the variability in the declared resistance.

The expressed resistance value is measured in Ohms. The tolerance is a percentage.

For example (after converting from colors to numbers), you might see bands that read the following values: 2, 7, 5, 5
That would mean the resistor's nominal resistance is 2700000 Ohms, with a tolerance of +/- 5%.
