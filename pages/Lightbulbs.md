- ### Types of Lightbulbs
	- <u>Incandescent Bulb</u>
	  An [incandescent bulb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incandescent_light_bulb) is one that produces light by heating a filament, which causes it to glow. Because the filament is being heated, this type of light bulb is not as energy efficient, since it loses some energy to heat.
	  
	  [Halogen bulbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halogen_lamp) are a specific type of incandescent bulb that is supposed to be a bit more energy efficient, with a light that is slightly more white.
	- <u>Compact Fluorescent Lamp</u>
	  A [compact fluorescent lamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_fluorescent_lamp) is much more energy efficient than incandescent bulbs.
	- <u>Light Emitting Diode</u>
	  See [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-emitting_diode).
- ### Lightbulb Sizing
	- <u>Screw Base Sizing</u>
	  Most household lightbulbs use an [Edison screw base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edison_screw), which are designated in the format "Exx". The E stands for Edison, and "xx" is a number indicating the diameter of the screw base in millimeters, measured across the peaks of the threads. For example, an E26 bulb has a screw base diameter of 26 millimeters.
	- <u>Bulb Enclosure Sizing</u>
	  Lightbulbs can also be qualified by the size of their glass bulb enclosure. Most bulbs follow the common pear-style shape, which is classified with an [A-series number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-series_light_bulb) in the format "Axx". The "xx" is a number indicating the diameter of the bulb enclosure at its widest point, and is either provided in 1/8 inches (North America) or in millimeters (rest of the world). For example, an A19/A60 bulb has a bulb diameter of 19/8 inches (or 60 millimeters).
	  
	  Sometimes, the "A" is replaced by a different letter to indicate a bulb shape that deviates from the traditional pear-style one. An example of this is ST64, which implies a Straight Tubular bulb with a diameter of 64 millimeters. A list of the other types of bulb shapes and their corresponding letters can be found [here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-series_light_bulb#Specifications).
- ### Lightbulb Brightness
	- A lightbulb's brightness is measured in [Lumens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumen_(unit)).
	- Note that per the [Weber-Fechner law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weber%E2%80%93Fechner_law), human perception of brightness is logarithmic.
		- In other words, we are more sensitive to brightness changes at lower brightness levels.
- ### Lightbulb Color Temperature
	- A lightbulb's [color temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_temperature) is measured in Kelvins.
