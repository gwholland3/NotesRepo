## Why Does Sunscreen Exist?
Always good to ask “why” first.

The sun emits electromagnetic radiation across a broad spectrum of wavelengths/frequencies, and this radiation can be harmful to our skin at certain wavelengths. Specifically, any radiation with a wavelength shorter than 400 nanometers poses a high risk of skin damage.

Note that visible light, which is a type of electromagnetic radiation, spans the wavelengths from 400 to 700 nm. That is, any radiation with a wavelength shorter than that of visible light is potentially harmful to us.

Luckily, the Earth’s atmosphere already does a pretty good job of protecting us from most of this harmful radiation. It completely absorbs the following radiation, thus preventing it from reaching our skin:
- Gamma rays, which have a wavelength less than 0.01 nm
- X-rays, which span the wavelengths from 0.01 to 10 nm

### UV Rays
That just leaves the wavelengths from 10 to 400 nm, which are generally labeled as ultraviolet (UV) radiation. UV radiation is itself divided up into several smaller wavelength bands:
- Extreme ultraviolet (EUV) radiation, which spans the wavelengths from 10 to 121 nm
- Ultraviolet C (UVC) radiation, which spans the wavelengths from 100 to 280 nm
- Ultraviolet B (UVB) radiation, which spans the wavelengths from 280 to 315 nm
- Ultraviolet A (UVA) radiation, which spans the wavelengths from 315 to 400 nm

The Earth’s atmosphere also helps us out with UV rays, although it only blocks some of them. Specifically, EUV and UVC radiation is completely absorbed by the atmosphere.

However, because of ozone depletion, Most UVA and UVB radiation can still reach the surface, and therefore our skin. This is where sunscreen comes in, to fill in that missing gap of skin protection against electromagnetic radiation.

## Effects of UV Rays
UVA and UVB rays have different effects on our skin.

UVA:
- Indirect DNA damage
- Accelerated skin aging
- Skin pigmentation

UVB:
- Direct DNA damage
- Sunburn
