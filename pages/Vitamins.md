Vitamins are a type of micronutrient. 

The key characteristic of a vitamin is that in general, our bodies either cannot synthesize it on our own at all, or we cannot synthesize a sufficient quantity to meet our needs. So, we must acquire them through foods.

They are also organic (as opposed to minerals, which are inorganic micronutrients). And because they are organic molecules, they can be broken down or degraded (this is why cooking can reduce the vitamin content in food).

There are two main types of vitamins:
- Fat-soluble vitamins dissolve in fat. Because of this, they are stored in fat tissues and the liver. Since we're prone to storing them, there is a risk of accumulating too much, which results in toxicity
- Water-soluble vitamins dissolve in water. Because of this, the body uses what it needs in the moment and flushes the rest out of the body through the kidneys in urine. Since we don't really store them, there is a risk of not regularly consuming enough, which results in deficiency

Because vitamins are needed in such small quantities, our bodies generally don't have any built-in mechanisms to make you crave certain foods if you are deficient in vitamins they contain, like we do with macronutrients (e.g. craving salt, sugar, or fat). This means you won't notice you're vitamin-deficient until it's gone on long enough to start affecting the metabolic processes that require those vitamins.

This is one of the reasons it's so important to consciously eat a diet that is rich in necessary vitamins.


## Vitamin A
Wikipedia: [link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_A)

Vitamin A is a fat-soluble vitamin.

It is most well-known for aiding vision, because it is a key component of the pigment rhodopsin. This pigment is essential for absorbing light and allowing your eyes to adjust to dim light.

Vitamin A also helps maintain epithelial tissue, e.g. skin. This means it has a direct tie to supporting your body's immune system, since epithelial tissue is one of our mechanisms for protecting against foreign entities.

### Sources
Vitamin A can be found in animal form, which is "active" aka ready to use, or plant form, which must be processed first.

Common animal sources:
- Animal liver (liver, liver oil)
- Animal dairy (butter, cheese)
- Egg products

Common plant sources:
- Sweet potato
- Carrot
- Pumpkin
- Spinach
- Kale

## Vitamin D
Wikipedia: [link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_D)

Vitamin D is also a fat-soluble vitamin.

Vitamin D can be synthesized in the skin when we're exposed to UV-B rays in sunlight. This is unusual for a vitamin - given that the body can produce Vitamin D itself, it is technically less of a traditional vitamin and more of a hormone.

It primarily increases the efficiency at which calcium can be absorbed into the small intestine from our food. Without adequate Vitamin D, we can only absorb 10-15% of the calcium we consume. With sufficient Vitamin D levels, that absorption rate jumps to 30-40%.

And if the body doesn't absorb sufficient calcium from food, it starts pulling calcium out of bones to keep blood calcium levels healthy, leading to bone softening.

### Sources
Vitamin D can be naturally synthesized through sunlight exposure.

Besides that, here are some natural food sources:
- Fatty fish are the best natural food sources
- Mushrooms can contain a good amount of Vitamin D, but only if treated with UV light
- Eggs have a small quantity

There are also some foods that are commonly fortified with Vitamin D in the US:
- Animal-based milk
- Plant-based milk
- Breakfast cereals
