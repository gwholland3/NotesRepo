What is smell? How do we smell things?

We smell things when various molecules enter our nose through the air. Once they get past the parts of the nose that are responsible for filtering the air before it reaches our lungs, they bind to a region at the back of the nose called the olfactory epithelium, which contains lots of smell receptors.

## Smell Management
How do you control smell, both your own and that of certain spaces or items?

Since smell boils down to the detection of specific volatile, airborne molecules by the human nose, there are a couple ways.

The simplest way is to eliminate the source of the smell. If whatever is originating the bad smell molecules can be removed, then the smell will disappear.

A second simple way to reduce smell is to distribute or clear it. Even if there is a constant source of smell in a location, if you can introduce air currents to spread the smell molecules out such that their concentration is low enough to avoid detection, then that works too. For example, if a room doesn’t smell great, but you can open a window and use a fan to spread outside air around, that should help.

Here are some other ways to control odors (from [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_freshener#Mechanisms_of_odor_control)):
- Masking: overwhelm undesired odors with stronger, desired ones
- Adsorption: when the odor molecules adhere to an introduced substance, thus removing them from air circulation
	- Trapping/absorption (similar to adsorption): when the odor molecules are bonded to and trapped *within* an introduced airborne substance, thus preventing their detection by human noses
	- Devolatilization (similar to adsorption: when the odor molecules are bonded to an introduced substance that makes the combined molecule too heavy to remain/become airborne
- Neutralization: introduce a substance that chemically alters the odor molecules to change their smell
- Disinfection: if the odors are created by organisms, killing the organisms will stop the odor production

### Human Sources of Smell
There are several sources of smell from humans, but the main ones are body odor and flatulence.

<u>Body Odor</u>
Body odor is mainly caused by the bacteria on your skin feeding off of the sweat you produce. Hence, body odor reduction primarily relies on two methods: reducing the number of bacteria on your skin, and reducing the amount of sweat that sits on your skin.

Anti-perspirants are products that you can apply to yourself, and they aim to reduce the amount of sweat you produce. They typically achieve this by blocking sweat glands, preventing the sweat from reaching the surface of your skin. However, there is mixed consensus on the health side effects of using aluminum-based anti-perspirants.

Deodorant attempts to kill the skin bacteria that are responsible for producing body odor.

Certain clothing and body wipes attempt to absorb sweat and moisture, to keep it off your skin and away from the bacteria. The drier your skin is, the better, from a body odor perspective.

Odor-causing bacteria can get attached to the clothing you wear, though, so washing your clothing will make sure it does not become a secondary source of body odor.

Even though your sweat itself doesn’t really have much of an odor, its composition influences the odorous byproducts that your skin bacteria produce when they feed on it. Because the compounds in your sweat are themselves influenced by the food you consume, changing your diet can change your natural body odor.

### Smell Management for Cars
This is mainly focused on car interiors.

<u>Fighting Odors</u>

Passive:
- Place simple deodorizers/dehumidifiers in your car as permanent fixtures, e.g. [baking soda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_bicarbonate#Odor_control) or activated charcoal
- Place a fragrance device in your car to mask undesired odors with more desirable ones, e.g. [little trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Trees)
Active:
- Circulation/Ventilation: open the windows while driving every once in a while, to dilute the concentration of odors
- Basic cleaning:
	- Vacuum to remove particles that could be odor sources
	- Wipe down surfaces with disinfectant to deactivate odor-causing organisms
- Periodically spray an airborne deodorizer throughout the interior air

<u>Preventing Odors</u>
- Keep a towel in the car to cover your seat when you are driving while sweaty/moist
	- Hang the towel out to dry/aerate after every time you use it, to prevent odor buildup in the towel itself
	- Periodically give the towel a full wash, since drying/aerating can only do so much after enough uses
- Minimize the amount of food you consume in the car
- Keep the car windows closed when in locations with undesirable odors

Source: [https://www.wikihow.com/Make-Your-Car-Smell-Good](https://www.wikihow.com/Make-Your-Car-Smell-Good)


### Smell Management for Shoes

Shoe odor is caused by the same thing as body odor: bacteria feeding off of sweat.

<u>Prevention</u>
Wear socks with your shoes. The socks will absorb moisture from your feet so that your shoes don't.

Mix up which pair of shoes you use, to avoid moisture accumulation in any one pair.

After you use shoes, help them dry out quickly by leaving them in a well-ventilated area. To further aid the process, you can add some of these products to your shoes after using them:
- Baking soda: sprinkle some in to absorb moisture, then dump out the used baking soda later
- Dryer sheets: put them in your shoes, then remove later

If you can, remove the insoles after use to help them and the rest of the shoes dry faster.

<u>Elimination</u>
If there is already sufficient odor buildup, you can wash your shoes to help remove some of the existing odor. Either machine wash if the shoes can handle that, or scrub with a soft brush using mild detergent and water. Make sure to let your shoes fully dry after washing them, before you start wearing them again.
