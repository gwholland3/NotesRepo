Things to remember about recycling, from an average citizen’s perspective.

## The Important Thing About Recycling
The most important thing to remember about recycling is that everything depends on who is actually collecting your recycling.

If you live in an area where there is no recycling service, then you quite simply cannot recycle anything, unless you go out of your way to find third-party recycling options.


## Recycling Services
Most recycling services are connected with a municipality, rather than being a completely private service. Once you’ve determined which recycling services you interact with, you can check what their specific rules are.


## General Recycling Guidelines
See below for general info to help with recycling

### Common Recycle Items
The following items will be accepted by almost any recycling service.

Paper:
- Newspapers
- Magazines
- Flattened cardboard
- Office paper
- Mail
- Paper bags

Plastics: see below for plastic recycling assessment via RICs

Metals:
- Aluminum cans
- Steel cans
- Tin cans

Glass:
- Bottles
- Jars

### Resin Identification Codes
RICs are numbers used to classify plastics. Here is the breakdown of the seven possible numbers:
1. PET (Polyethylene Terephthalate)
	* Common Uses: Soda bottles, water bottles, some food packaging.
	* Recyclability: Widely accepted in curbside recycling programs.
2. HDPE (High-Density Polyethylene)
	* Common Uses: Milk jugs, detergent bottles, juice bottles.
	* Recyclability: Widely accepted in curbside recycling programs.
3. PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride)
	* Common Uses: Plumbing pipes, shrink wrap, some food packaging.
	* Recyclability: Not commonly accepted in curbside recycling programs. Requires special handling.
4. LDPE (Low-Density Polyethylene)
	* Common Uses: Plastic bags, bread bags, some food wraps.
	* Recyclability: Not commonly accepted in curbside recycling programs, but some stores have collection bins for plastic bags.
5. PP (Polypropylene)
	* Common Uses: Yogurt containers, straws, bottle caps, some food containers.
	* Recyclability: Increasingly accepted in curbside recycling programs, but still varies by location.
6. PS (Polystyrene)
	* Common Uses: Styrofoam cups, take-out containers, egg cartons.
	* Recyclability: Rarely accepted in curbside recycling programs due to difficulty in processing and low market value.
7. Other (Various Plastics)
	* Common Uses: A variety of plastics not included in the other categories, including bioplastics.
	* Recyclability: Varies widely; some types are recyclable, but others are not.

To summarize: #1 and #2 are the most widely accepted types of plastic for recycling, and #5 is also becoming more widespread.

### Other Tips
- Clean items before you recycle them, i.e. if they contain food residue
- Remove caps and lids, which are not generally recyclable
